# Campus Customs — Design Notes (Problem 10)

Creative design pass so the site feels like a real Campus Customs storefront. What we changed
and why it helps customers stay and buy.

## Hero: Yale campus in fall behind a navy gradient
**What:** The homepage hero shows an aerial photo of Yale's campus in autumn (Harkness Tower /
the residential-college quads) under a navy-blue gradient, with the "Big Pride, Big Yale."
headline and shop buttons on top. A subtle fade-up animation plays on load.
**Why it helps:** A real, aspirational Yale scene makes the store feel official and local
within the first second, building trust. The gradient keeps the headline and call-to-action
readable, so visitors immediately know what the site is and where to shop.

## Mascot: Handsome Dan as the chat logo
**What:** The chat helper is now **Handsome Dan**, Yale's bulldog mascot (image used for the
floating chat button and the chat header), replacing the generic CSS mascot. Copy across the
site refers to "Handsome Dan."
**Why it helps:** A recognizable, friendly Yale mascot invites shoppers to open the chat and
ask for help — more chats mean more guided discovery and more completed purchases. It also
reinforces the brand on every page.

## Typography: Times New Roman throughout
**What:** The whole site uses Times New Roman (serif) for headings and body.
**Why it helps:** A classic serif reads as collegiate and traditional — on-brand for an Ivy
League store — giving the storefront a more established, premium feel than a generic sans-serif.

## Supporting polish (hierarchy, motion, product presentation, chat feel)
- **Hierarchy:** clear hero → "Shop by category" → product grid flow; prices and product names
  are emphasized on cards so shoppers scan quickly.
- **Motion:** cards lift on hover and the hero content fades up on load — small cues that make
  the site feel responsive and alive without being distracting.
- **Product presentation:** uniform square product images, color/price emphasis, and sold-out
  badges so availability is honest at a glance.
- **Category thumbnails:** each "Shop by category" card shows a representative product photo
  (hoodie, tee, quarter-zip, jacket), normalized to a white background and a uniform square
  size (320×320), so shoppers instantly see what each category looks like and the row stays
  tidy and consistent.
- **Chat feel:** Markdown-formatted replies (bold + bullets), a typing indicator, and product
  cards rendered inline make the helper feel like a real, capable store associate.

_Net effect:_ the site looks like a legitimate, polished Yale storefront, which keeps visitors
browsing longer, builds the trust needed to buy, and nudges them toward the chat helper that
drives discovery.

## Why these choices (store owner's rationale)
- **Times New Roman** matches Yale's official website, so the store reads as official,
  licensed merch rather than a generic shop — that credibility makes shoppers more comfortable
  buying.
- **Category photos** were added because the original version had none — a product picture
  immediately shows customers what we actually sell, which matters for a shop. They were also
  chosen as a mix of navy, white, and grey, so the first glance feels varied and the range of
  products feels broad and appealing.
- **The Yale fall campus photo** blended into the hero puts shoppers on campus in their
  imagination — picturing themselves wearing Campus Customs gear there — which builds the
  emotional pull to buy.
